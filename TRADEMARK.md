# Charter Trademark Policy

The Charter source code is licensed under the AGPL-3.0. **The Charter name and logo are not.**

Open source licenses cover copyright, not trademarks. This document explains what you may and may not do with the name.

The purpose is narrow: people should be able to tell whether the Charter they are running came from this project. Nothing here is intended to restrict your use of the software itself, which the AGPL already governs.

---

## You may, without asking

- **State that your software uses, is built on, or is compatible with Charter.** Factual, descriptive references are always fine.
- Run Charter, modified or unmodified, for any purpose the AGPL permits.
- Use the name in blog posts, talks, tutorials, comparisons, and reviews — including critical ones.
- Use the logo to link to this project.
- Distribute unmodified Charter under its own name.
- Say "we self-host Charter" on your own site.

## You may not, without written permission

- **Name a fork, distribution, or derivative work "Charter"**, or a name confusingly similar to it. Rebrand it.
- **Offer a hosted or commercial service under the Charter name.** This includes names like "Charter Cloud," "Charter Hosted," "ChartAI," or similar constructions.
- Use the name or logo in a way suggesting this project endorses, sponsors, or is affiliated with your product.
- Register the name, or a confusingly similar name, as a trademark or domain in any jurisdiction.
- Modify the logo and continue to present it as the Charter logo.

## Modified versions

If you distribute a modified Charter, you must:

1. Give it a distinct name that is not confusingly similar to Charter.
2. Not use the Charter logo as your project's primary mark.
3. Clearly state that your project is a fork of, or derived from, Charter — this is encouraged, and is a descriptive use covered above.

**Example:** naming a fork "Ledger, a fork of Charter" is fine. Naming it "Charter Pro" is not.

---

## Why this exists

The AGPL is a copyright license. It requires anyone running a modified Charter as a network service to publish their modifications, but it cannot stop someone from launching a competing product under this project's name and reputation.

This policy is the piece that does that. It is deliberately permissive about everything else — descriptive use, criticism, forks under their own names, and commercial self-hosting are all explicitly fine.

## Questions

If your intended use isn't clearly covered here, ask. Requests to use the name in ways not permitted above are considered case by case and are not automatically refused.

Open a discussion, or contact the maintainer directly.
